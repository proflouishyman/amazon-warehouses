# INTERP ENV: netting Amazon's logistics buildings and delivery against displaced stores and shopping trips

> **Bottom line.** Amazon's US warehouses and deliveries produce roughly 4 to 5 million tonnes of CO2e a year by our estimate (4.4 Mt in 2022, 5.3 Mt in 2025), and probably more, because air, FC-to-FC transfers and the van stem leg are not counted. Whether that is more or less than the store energy and shopping trips they replace depends on one number nobody has measured: how much in-store shopping each online dollar actually removes. On our assumptions the network breaks even if each dollar spent online cuts about **30 to 95 cents** of in-store spending (central about 60 cents), together with the car trips that go with it. Studies of grocery shopping suggest online orders replace some store spending and store trips but add other trips, and they do not settle whether the real figure is above or below that line. We therefore cannot say whether Amazon's logistics network raises or lowers US emissions overall. (The 30 to 95 cents is the spread when one assumption at a time is moved to its low or high value; stacking every assumption at its limit gives 7 to 510 cents, which is not an uncertainty range.)


Built 2026-11-01. Script: `python3 -I scripts/interp_env_netting.py` (standard library only; reads the CSVs named below and writes only `data/processed/interp_env_*.csv`). Every output is an ESTIMATE from public inputs and stated assumptions. Nothing here measures Amazon's facilities or deliveries, and nothing here is a finding about how much retail Amazon displaced. Status key: SOURCED (read from the filing or file in this session), DERIVED (arithmetic on sourced values), ASSUMPTION (a stated parameter, not sourced), PLACEHOLDER (to be replaced).

## 2. Building energy per $1,000 of sales (`interp_env_building_intensity_per_sales.csv`)

Gas 53.06 kg per mmBtu is EPA GHG Hub 2025 Table 1 (CO2 only).

Reading: per dollar, Amazon's network uses roughly a third of Walmart's store energy and under a fifth of Target's, driven by fewer sq ft per dollar (about 1.06 per $1,000 of GMV, 1.19 per $1,000 of revenue, versus 1.45 Walmart, 2.39 Target) and lower per-sq-ft intensity. Adding DCs raises the store retailers by about 13% (Walmart, proxy) and 13% (Target), so the store-versus-warehouse gap shrinks only a little. Even at retail-level intensity Amazon's central (5.98 per GMV) stays below Walmart stores (8.22) and Target (13.58), but the ranges overlap: Amazon's high warehouse case (5.91 per GMV, 6.70 per revenue) is above the Walmart low case (5.20). **CBECS warehouse intensity likely understates Amazon's automated, conditioned fulfilment centres** (CBECS warehouses average 17,400 sq ft, many unconditioned; Amazon publishes no kWh), so the warehouse central is a floor and the retail-intensity row is the more cautious comparison. Per-dollar intensity falls over time for all firms (nominal sales growth, cleaner grid). Boundaries differ: Walmart sales include eCommerce picked in stores, and neither store energy nor Amazon building energy covers vehicles, data centres or corporate offices.

## 3. Transport per item (`interp_env_transport_per_item.csv`, `interp_env_transport_breakeven.csv`, `interp_env_transport_literature.csv`)

One store outing against one package. Revised 2026-11-01 after an adversarial review.

- **Store outing** = 2 legs x 5.8 miles x car factor x chaining. One survey year (NHTS 2022) is now used for the length (5.8 miles, margin of error 0.4, Table 3-5, printed p.20). The 2017 length (7.0) is a separate sensitivity only. Car factor 0.2985 / 0.3476 / 0.3966 kg CO2e per mile (EPA GHG Hub 2025 Table 8). The 2 legs are right for an outing, because an NHTS person trip is one leg (see section 4).
- **Delivery per package** = van miles per package x van kg per mile + truck miles per parcel path x 1.308 kg per truck-mile / parcels per trailer. Van factor: low 0.48 (FHWA average light truck/van 18.5 mpg x 8.887 kg per gallon; AFDC page citing FHWA Highway Statistics 2024 Table VM-1; the page prints names and values separately so the pairing is inferred from order), high 1.24 (FHWA delivery truck 7.19 mpg), central 0.60 = ASSUMPTION for a loaded stop-start cargo van (the review's figure; no published Amazon value). Middle mile uses EPA's vehicle-mile factor (heavy truck 1.298 kg CO2 + CH4 and N2O = 1.308), not a ton-mile factor, because parcel trailers fill by volume; parcels per trailer 4,000 / 3,000 / 2,000 and 150 / 300 / 600 truck miles are ASSUMPTIONS. Last-mile miles per package 0.4 / 0.67 / 1.2 are ASSUMPTIONS (no Amazon figure). **Returns are not in the central case**; air is not modelled in the central case (flagged missing; see sensitivities).
- Three coherent cases (online-favourable, central, store-favourable) are kept for the basket table only.

Results (no returns): store outing 4.92 / 4.03 / 1.61 kg CO2e; delivery 0.24 / 0.53 / 1.88 kg per package (central last mile 0.40, middle mile 0.13). At an 8-item basket: store 0.61 / 0.50 / 0.20 kg per item vs delivery 0.24 / 0.53 / 1.88. **Break-even basket** (items per outing below which online is lower): 20.4 / 7.6 / 0.9 items, falling to 15.7 / 5.8 / 0.7 with a 30% return rate (15%: 17.7 / 6.6 / 0.75). ## 4. The break-even substitution rate (revised 2026-11-01; `interp_env_breakeven_sensitivity.csv`)

This replaces the earlier attribution that netted a level (kt) of Amazon's emissions against a 2010-2022 increment of displaced sales. The review showed that design charged Amazon for all of its 2022 GMV ($375bn) but credited it only for the increment in five categories ($148bn), inflating the break-even about 2.5 times, and that the shopping-trip emissions were overstated 2.6 to 7.5 times. Both are fixed by putting both sides on the same base, **a dollar of Amazon online sales**:

- Own emissions per $1,000 of Amazon NA GMV (2022 central): buildings **2.92** + delivery **8.88** = **11.80 kg CO2e**. Buildings: 394.0M sq ft x (5.8 kWh x grid 2022 + 11.38 kBtu gas) / GMV $374.8bn. Delivery: 0.533 kg per package / $60 per package (ASSUMPTION) x 1,000.
- Avoided emissions per $1,000 of IN-STORE spend removed (sigma = 1): trips **12.41** + buildings **7.29** = **19.71 kg**. Trips: NHTS shopping VMT of 47.6 miles per $1,000 of in-store sales x 0.3476 kg per mile x (1 - 0.25 offset). Buildings: 0.5 realisation x store sq ft per dollar x CBECS retail intensity (Walmart-Target mean 1.96 sq ft per $1,000; 6.03 kg per sq ft) for the retail mix, and the $749 per sq ft supermarket figure (Economic Census 2017) with CBECS food-sales intensity (22.5 kg per sq ft) for grocery, mixed 85 / 15 using the interp_spend 2010-2022 increment of online dollars as the mix weight.
- **Break-even sigma = own / avoided = 11.80 / 19.71 = 0.60**: Amazon's network breaks even if each online dollar removes 60 cents of in-store spending (with its trips and building space), for the Amazon-average dollar. There is no central net and no Amazon-share step: with both sides per Amazon online dollar, Amazon's share of e-commerce cancels. The 2010 baseline is dropped because sigma already says what fraction of an online dollar replaces an in-store dollar (the rest is new consumption).

**Trip calibration (review B2).** Shopping-trip emissions are now tied to NHTS vehicle-miles, not to trip counts x legs. (a) Return legs: an NHTS person trip is "movement from one point to another" and a shopping-and-errand count exists only by purpose; Table 3-2 purposes (366, 70, 337, 364, 225, 588, 40) add exactly to All (1,990), with no home column, so legs returning home are inside the purposes and the 0.80 trips per person per day already includes both legs (FHWA-HPL-24-009, Table 3-2 printed p.19, definitions in Appendix D "Person trip", "Travel day trip", "Vehicle trip"). The NHTS text does not state in so many words that return-home legs are counted under the activity purpose; this is an inference from the table arithmetic and the glossary, which should be confirmed in the 2022 NHTS derived-variables memo. (b) Person trips are not car trips: 229 shopping vehicle trips against 337 person trips (0.68), vehicle occupancy 1.53 (Table 3-6 printed p.21; Table 5-2 printed p.41). (c) One survey year: 2022 length and VMT. (d) Reconciliation (`interp_env_trip_reconciliation.csv`): households 127.5M (Table 4-1 / Table 3-2); shopping VMT 1,320 per household (Table 3-4 printed p.20) = 168bn miles; shopping plus errands 451bn; VMT per shopping vehicle trip 5.76 miles (Table 3-5: 5.8). The first run implied 1,265bn vehicle-miles, 2.8 times shopping-plus-errands and 7.5 times shopping-only. The in-store sales base is Census ARTS 2022 benchmarked total retail $6,975bn minus motor vehicle and parts $1,576bn, gasoline $722bn and nonstore retailers $1,142bn = **$3,535bn** (verified from `https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/arts/tables/2022benchmarked/sales.xlsx`; the review's recalled $3.55 tn was right to 0.4%). Errands (post office, dry cleaner, library) are not retail purchases, so adding their VMT is an upper-bound sensitivity only. NHTS 2017 shopping VMT per household (2,618) over 2017 in-store sales ($2,728bn) gives 113 miles per $1,000, 2.4 times the 2022 figure; NHTS 2022 has a method break and pandemic effects, so this is the largest single uncertainty on the avoided side.

**Sensitivity, one assumption at a time** (break-even sigma; central 0.60). Own-side: van 0.48 / 1.24 -> 0.53 / 0.96; van miles per package 0.4 / 1.2 -> 0.46 / 0.87; middle mile 150 / 600 miles -> 0.54 / 0.71; parcels per trailer 4,000 / 2,000 -> 0.57 / 0.65; USD per package $90 / $40 -> 0.45 / 0.82; Amazon buildings at warehouse low / high -> 0.54 / 0.77; Amazon buildings at retail intensity -> 0.77; returns 15% / 30% -> 0.67 / 0.73; air 5% of packages at 1,000 miles -> 0.67; own year 2025 -> 0.59. Avoided-side: trip offset 0.5 / 0 -> 0.76 / 0.50; store energy realisation 0.25 / 1.0 -> 0.74 / 0.44; car factor 0.2985 / 0.3966 -> 0.66 / 0.55; store density Walmart / Target -> 0.62 / 0.58; no grocery / 30% grocery in the mix -> 0.65 / 0.56; shopping + errands VMT -> 0.29; NHTS 2017 basis -> 0.32. **Range across one-at-a-time rows: 0.29 to 0.96.** The two lowest values come from the VMT bases that include errands or the 2017 survey; excluding them the range is 0.44 to 0.96. **Stacked corners** (separate rows, labelled NOT a range): all assumptions favourable to Amazon at once 0.065; all unfavourable 5.1.

**Illustration only** (`interp_env_net_illustration_only.csv`): central own emissions 4.4 Mt (2022); avoided if sigma were 0.05 / 0.10 / 0.20 / 0.30 / 0.40 / 0.60 / 1.00: 0.4 / 0.7 / 1.5 / 2.2 / 3.0 / 4.4 / 7.4 Mt, so net +4.1 / +3.7 / +2.9 / +2.2 / +1.5 / 0.0 / -3.0 Mt. These are labelled values, not findings; 1.0 is an upper bound not supported by the literature. No single net or sign is claimed.

**Evidence strip** (`interp_env_evidence_strip.csv`), shown beside the break-even and not turned into a central sigma. Pozzi 2013: under $0.40 of the chain's own in-store grocery spend per online dollar; this is own-chain cannibalisation, so economy-wide displacement for groceries is at least this and plausibly more (not a ceiling). Chava et al.: nearby store sales fall 4% after a fulfilment centre opens; a percent of store sales, not a per-dollar rate, so it cannot be placed on the sigma axis. Relihan 2022: grocery adopters make 7.4% fewer grocery-store trips but more trips to coffee shops, restaurants, pharmacies; trips, not dollars, motivating the 25% trip offset. The 0.60 break-even lies above Pozzi's own-chain 0.40, i.e. above any grocery-only evidence in hand, but the literature does not cover general merchandise, where the dollars are.

**Population (re-fetched from Census 2026-11-01).** July 1, 2022 = 333,287,557 (`https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/datasets/2020-2022/state/totals/NST-EST2022-ALLDATA.csv`, POPESTIMATE2022); July 1, 2017 = 324,985,539 (`https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/datasets/2010-2019/national/totals/nst-est2019-alldata.csv`, POPESTIMATE2017). The model no longer uses population; it appears in the reconciliation (0.735 shopping-and-errand person trips per person per day from the national total vs NHTS Table 4-5's 0.80).

Amazon share and categories: category shares and PCE from interp_spend now serve only as mix weights (grocery 15% of the 2010-2022 online-dollar increment). Department-store, general-merchandise and pharmacy lines are missing from the mix (suppressed e-commerce), and the furniture and books ratios are inflated (carried in `interp_env_category_params.csv`); because only the mix matters, the effect is modest (no grocery 0.65, 30% grocery 0.56).

## 5. Scale against Amazon's reported emissions (`interp_env_scale_vs_reported.csv`)

Which scope: Amazon's methodology (carbon-methodology PDF, p.2) lists, within its operational-control boundary, "Amazon's last mile delivery fleet", "Amazon-operated freight, including trucks and airplanes" and "Purchased delivery services (e.g., postal services) and other contracted freight"; the 2023 sustainability report (PDF p.11) says Scope 1 is "primarily generated from the fuel used by our transportation and logistics fleet to deliver packages to customers". Neither document names DSP-operated vans explicitly, so that DSP vans are in Scope 1 is an INFERENCE (Amazon's own fleet, air and trucks appear to be Scope 1; postal and third-party carriers are Scope 3 category 4). Scope placement does not affect this system-level comparison.

Corrected check: NA is about 73% of retail-segment sales in 2022 (NA / (NA + International), 315,880 / 433,887), so NA's share of the 12.6 Mt global Scope 1 fossil fuels is about 9.2 Mt. The bottom-up Scope-1-like total for NA (delivery 3.33 Mt + building gas 0.24 Mt = 3.57 Mt in 2022; 4.35 Mt in 2025) is 0.39 to 0.55 of that top-down marker in every year 2019-2025. **The bottom-up is well below what Amazon's reported Scope 1 implies for North America, so it is likely a floor** (missing air, FC-to-FC transfers, van stem leg, refrigerants, stores; the top-down also includes non-logistics fuel). Location-based building electricity (0.86 Mt in 2022) is shown separately. The earlier statement that the bottom-up supported the order of magnitude is withdrawn. If own emissions were 2.5 times higher (the top-down ratio), the central break-even would be about 1.5, i.e. no plausible substitution rate would net it out; the stacked pessimistic corner (5.1) shows the same.

## 6. Gaps and cautions
1. Break-even is for the Amazon-average dollar. Category differences in Amazon's own per-dollar emissions and in how much in-store spend each category removes are not modelled; interp_spend gives no Amazon share by category.
2. Delivery inputs (miles per package, parcels per trailer, haul, $ per package, central van factor 0.60) are assumptions, not Amazon data; air is absent; the bottom-up Scope-1-like total is 39-55% of the top-down marker.
3. The NHTS return-leg convention is inferred (section 4); 2022 NHTS has a method break and gives 2.4 times fewer shopping miles per dollar than 2017.
4. In-store base is ARTS 2022; Walmart DC sq ft is a proxy (section 1); Target DC definition changes in FY2022; NA includes Canada, Mexico and digital media (packages overstated perhaps 5-10%).
5. No public national retail floor-space series: avoided building energy rests on Walmart and Target sq ft per dollar and a 25-100% realisation assumption.
6. CBECS is 2018 and warehouse means include unconditioned buildings; US-average grid.
7. Returns and air are sensitivities only; store-side returns are not modelled.
8. Pozzi's number is own-chain and grocery only; Chava is not in per-dollar units; Relihan is grocery trips; no study gives general-merchandise substitution per online dollar. Rebound consumption is inside sigma.
9. The substituted per-package value ($60) and the 3P share (58%) also move the own side through the GMV base (3P share only moves GMV, hence only the level, not the per-dollar break-even).

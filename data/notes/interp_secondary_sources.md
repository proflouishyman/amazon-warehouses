# Secondary literature on Amazon's US logistics network: how the arguments are made

Compiled 2026-11-01 for the interpretation page. Public sources only. Raw copies are in `data/raw/interp_secondary/` (gitignored; PDF plus a `.txt` extraction beside each; HTML pages kept as saved).
Scope: the Senate HELP 2024 report, Amazon's safety and Climate Pledge statements, OSHA enforcement, and the Warehouse CITY / Consumer Reports studies were collected by another agent and are only cross-referenced here.

Conventions. "Opened" means the full document was saved and read for the fields below. "Page" is the printed page number where visible; "~" means approximate (section named instead where the PDF layout hid the number). "Not disclosed" means no funding statement in the document or on the org page I could open. Figures are as the source states them; I did not recompute any of them here.

Two corrections to the brief: the 2018 EPI county-employment report is by Janelle Jones and Ben Zipperer (not Jacobs). "Amazon's Disposable Workers" (CPD) could not be found; see Gaps.

## 2. How the arguments are made

### 2.1 Recurring data sources

1. OSHA ITA Form 300A establishment filings: S1, S2, S8, S9, S10 (via SOC), and Amazon's own safety claims. The same file is read by both sides; differences are comparator, years and NAICS filter, not the data.
2. Internal Amazon injury logs obtained outside ITA: S3 (worker requests under OSHA record access rules, 28 facilities, convenience sample) and S4 (Reveal, 23 of 110 FCs). Strong for texture, weak for generalisation, and both authors say so in their own text.
3. Worker surveys: S1 (n=996), S5 (n=1,484). They measure prevalence over a career, not annual incidence; only S5 says so explicitly.
4. BLS: SOII (S3, S8 for benchmarks), CES (S25), OES for wages (S15), QCEW for county jobs and earnings (S14, S25, S28). QCEW suppression for single-employer cells limits S25 (60 counties) and S14.
5. Modelled or consultancy estimates: S20 (ACS-driven), S26 (Keystone input-output), S17 (analyst margins). These dominate the economic-impact and environmental claims.
6. Subsidy Tracker (S18, S19): cumulative counts with "at least" language.
7. Company disclosures read against themselves: Amazon sustainability reports and CDP (S22, S24), 10-K and EEO-1 headcounts (S15, S8).

### 2.2 Comparator choice and how far it moves the conclusion

Injury rate, recordables per 100 FTE, similar years (all from S1, S2, S8, S3, Amazon via S8):

| Comparator | Amazon / comparator | Source |
|---|---|---|
| BLS 1,000+ employee warehouse category (Amazon's own choice), 2022: 6.8 vs Amazon 6.9 | about 1.0x | S8 quoting Amazon's report |
| Non-Amazon 1,000+ employee NAICS 493110 sites, 2023: 3.8 vs Amazon 6.5 | 1.71x | S8 |
| Non-Amazon ITA warehouses, 2020: 4.0 vs 6.5; 2021: 4.0 vs 7.7 | 1.6x; 1.9x | S1, S2 |
| BLS general warehousing 2018: 5.2 vs sampled Amazon FCs 10.76 | 2.1x | S3 |
| Walmart, 2020: 3.0 vs 6.5 (S1); 2023: 2.3 vs 6.5 (S8) | 2.2x; 2.8x | S1, S8 |
| All private employers 2018: 2.8 vs 10.76 | 3.8x | S3 |

The spread, from about 1.0x to 3.8x, is almost entirely a function of the benchmark. The 1,000+ benchmark is the weakest comparator because Amazon is 79% of that group's employment (S8). The Walmart comparison is the most favourable to the critics' side and the least like-for-like in mix (Walmart's warehouses are shipping to its own stores). The defensible middle is "non-Amazon peers, same NAICS, same size band", which gives 1.6x to 1.9x (S1, S2, S8). Severity classification adds a second axis: S2 itself shows Amazon's lost-time share fell from 78% to 35% while "serious" (lost time plus light duty) rose, and Amazon's reported reply (S4) is that conservative restricted-duty practice inflates its rate. The disagreement is partly about what to count.

Wage and jobs comparators flip the sign:

| Claim | Comparator | Direction and size | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amazon pays less | Comparable BLS occupation (53-7062), 11 MSAs, web-posted Amazon wages | -15% (-9% nationally) | S15 |
| Amazon pays more | Brick-and-mortar retail jobs in same county, QCEW weekly earnings | +31% (+33% in Amazon counties) | S25 |
| Amazon's effect on warehouse workers' earnings | County's own pre-trend (event study) | -1.7% to +0.5%, not significant | S14 |
| Amazon's effect on metro wages | Not-yet-treated metros | +0.7%, borderline | S28 |
| Jobs: net effect | Amazon payroll vs modelled displaced store jobs | -148,774 (2015) | S15 |
| Jobs: net effect | NAICS 493 + 4541 growth vs store FTE decline | +400,000 vs -140,000 | S25 |
| Jobs: net effect | Counties without openings | warehousing +30%, overall 0 | S14 |
| Jobs: net effect | Similar counties without investment (Amazon's team) | +1.7% employment-to-population; multiplier 1.9 | S27 |
| Local retail | Control counties | retail worker income -2.4%, closures +3 pp | S29 |

Wage comparison against warehouse labour (S15) and against retail (S25) are both correct as arithmetic and answer different questions: what Amazon pays relative to other warehouse employers versus relative to the retail job it competes with. Neither is a counterfactual for a given worker. The three counterfactual-based studies (S14, S28, S29) all find small or null wage effects and modest net job effects, which sits between S15 and S25.

### 2.3 Rhetorical and argumentative patterns

- Headline ratio ("more than double"): S1, S2, S3, S4, S8, S10, S11. The ratio is real but depends on which comparator is picked (table above). Only S8 tests the benchmark itself.
- Anecdote-led openings: S4 (Dixon), S12, S27 (Licking Co. and Arapahoe Co. examples in the company blog). Both sides use named individuals or counties to carry an aggregate claim.
- Cumulative counts without denominators: S18 ($241M), S19 ($4.18B US, $4.7B global), S26 ($1.8T GDP, $340B invested). None divides by jobs, output or time at risk in the document body.
- Counterfactual assumed rather than estimated: S15 (each Amazon sale displaces store sales at average productivity), S25 (all NAICS 493 growth is e-commerce), S26 (gross input-output effects). S14, S28, S29 estimate a counterfactual with controls.
- Causal language from correlation: S1 (robots and speed "contributing"), S2 (production quotas), S10 (labels), S27 (the company's own text says "strong correlation" while its headline says "boosts"). Survey-based claims (S5, S1) run correlation between pace and pain, with self-reports on both sides.
- Cumulative versus annual measures: S5 compares 41% ever injured with a 6.7% annual rate; the authors flag it but headline coverage may not.
- Selective year or scope: S1 uses 2020 (the COVID-slowdown year, which the authors disclose); S4 uses 23 of 110 FCs; S9 is one state; Amazon's own page uses the 24% improvement since 2019 (quoted in NELP's summary of Amazon's report, not opened) while SOC picks 2021 peaks.
- Boundary and scope arguments (environment): S22 and S24 argue over what counts (own brand about 1% of sales; sellers excluded; commutes excluded; intensity versus absolute). Amazon's reply is that sellers "control their own accounting" (S24).
- Source quality varies within each camp. The strongest critic work (S14, S5, S8, S28/S29 academic, S15's explicit methods) states its limits; the strongest pro work (S28, S29) is academic and finds mixed effects. The weakest on each side is the one that presents a modelled or curated figure as a count (S10 list ranking, S26 impact totals).

### 2.4 Where critics and Amazon agree on the numbers but disagree on framing

- Amazon's overall recordable rate: Amazon's reported 6.9 (2022) and 6.5 (2023) are the same numbers SOC and NELP use (S8). The dispute is the benchmark (all large warehouses at 6.8, which is mostly itself, versus non-Amazon peers at 3.8) and the label ("meaningful progress", 24% down since 2019 in Amazon's framing; "still 71% higher" in NELP's).
- Why the rate is high: S4 records Amazon's own explanation that its conservative return-to-work and aggressive recording raise restricted-duty and recordable counts, while critics (S1-S2, S5) read the same counts as hazard and underreporting. Both can be true; ITA cannot separate them.
- Emissions totals: S22 and S24 use Amazon's own reported totals (2022: 71.27 Mt in S22); the argument is about the boundary excluding seller products and commutes.
- Local jobs: S14 reports the +30% warehousing effect that Amazon cites in spirit; the dispute is whether total county employment moves. Amazon's county study (S27) and Cunningham (S28) find positive total employment effects with different designs and samples than S14 (counties 2001-2015 versus metros 2010-2020), so the studies are partly measuring different places and years rather than contradicting.
- Subsidies: S18 and S19 count dollars; Amazon's 2025 page cites jobs and investment but not subsidies. They do not contradict on the count, only on whether it was needed.

Reading the table: per square foot, a retail store uses about 2.4 times the warehouse's electricity (13.7 vs 5.8) and about 1.5 times the gas (16.75 vs 11.38 kBtu, DERIVED); malls about 3.4 times the electricity and about 3.9 times the gas. Total site energy per sq ft is 2.1 times (retail) and 3.7 times (malls) the warehouse.
Caveats: (a) The electricity and gas cu-ft columns use only buildings that use that fuel (conditional intensity); the DERIVED kBtu row divides by all floorspace, so it is not directly comparable to the cu-ft row. The warehouse 11.38 kBtu/sq ft matches the figure already in `environment.md`. (b) "Warehouse and storage" is all warehouses (mean 17.4 thousand sq ft, many unconditioned) and the electricity mean 5.8 is the already-used figure; Amazon fulfillment centers are much larger and automated, so this likely understates them (see environment.md). Retail intensities are for typical stores, not big boxes or closed department stores, which are bigger and probably have lower intensity per sq ft than strip stores. (c) "Enclosed mall" is a tiny survey sample (about 1 thousand buildings, 684 million sq ft) and its intensities are unreliable; the "Enclosed and strip malls" row is dominated by strip centers (4,904 of 5,588 million sq ft). (d) Per square foot is not per dollar of sales or per item; a closed store's energy is only avoided if the building is demolished or left unconditioned (vacant space still uses energy). (e) Relative standard errors are not shown in the xlsx rows used.

## 6. Gaps and what would be needed
- A full annual series of US store closures and openings 2010 to 2025: NOT found publicly. Coresight is paywalled; public numbers exist for 2020, 2023, 2024 (original and revised), 2025 only. CBP establishment stock is the only complete public annual series.
- National retail square footage by year: NOT found in public sources (only metro per-capita CoStar snippets and Coresight closure square-foot estimates).
- Per-order emissions for US general merchandise including returns: not found; opened evidence is UK FMCG (Shahmohammadi) and a San Francisco model (poster).
- Census County Business Patterns 2024 (NAICS 2022, sectors 455 and 458) was not retrieved.

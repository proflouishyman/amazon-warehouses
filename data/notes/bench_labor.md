# bench_labor: labor benchmark, Amazon vs Walmart/Sam's, Target, Costco, Home Depot, UPS, FedEx and NAICS 493

All retrieval dates 2026-10-31. Raw files: data/raw/benchmark/labor/{sec,wages,nlrb}/. The scripts that produced the NLRB tables are in data/raw/benchmark/labor/nlrb/ (crawl_by_year.py, extend_crawl.py, parse_nlrb.py, build_counts.py). Amazon series are reused from the existing files (amazon_employees_by_year.csv, amazon_wage_announcements.csv, nlrb_amazon_cases_search_index.csv); notes in labor.md.

## Headline comparisons
1. Amazon's announced average pay vs warehousing as a whole. Amazon "average pay over $23" (Sept 2025) is 84% of the QCEW NAICS 493 40-hour equivalent ($1,091/week = $27.27/h, 2025). The ratio has been steady at 72-84% since 2018 (bench_labor_announced_vs_qcew493.csv, Amazon rows). Amazon's "total compensation incl. benefits over $30" is 110% of it. Caveat: QCEW includes overtime, bonuses, managers and part-timers, and /40 is a proxy, so an announced front-line average and the QCEW mean are not like-for-like.
2. Peer wages next to the QCEW 493 mean (same file). Walmart supply chain average $27.50 = 101% (2025); Walmart DC starting range $16-$34 (Aug 2023) low end 64%; Target starting $15-$24 = 55% (low end, 2025); Home Depot floor $15 = 60% (2023); Costco minimum $20 = 73% and average "more than $31" (US+Canada, secondary source) = 114% (2025); UPS part-time start $21-$23 under the 2023 Teamsters contract = 84% (was $15.50, 62%, under the prior contract); UPS top full-time driver rate $49 = 197% (drivers, not warehouse). Amazon's average pay is therefore below Walmart's supply chain average and below Costco's average, and level with UPS's new part-time starting pay.
3. Employment (bench_labor_employees_by_year.csv; Amazon in amazon_employees_by_year.csv). Latest 10-K headcounts: Walmart 2.1M worldwide, 1.6M US (FY ended Jan 2026); Amazon 1,576,000 worldwide (FY2025); Home Depot 472,400 (US, Canada, Mexico); UPS 460,000 worldwide, 370,000 US (FY2025, down from 406,000 US in 2024); Target 415,000 (Jan 2026); Costco 355,000 worldwide, 232,000 US (Aug 2026); FedEx has no company-wide total after FY2021 (see gaps). Amazon's worldwide headcount is close to Walmart's US headcount (1.58M vs 1.6M) and it is the only company here that grew fast: 798,000 (2019) to 1,576,000 (2025).
4. Union representation (bench_labor_union_representation.csv). UPS: nearly 80% of US employees under Teamsters agreements (FY2025 10-K; more than 75% in FY2024; 327,000 covered in 2021). Costco: about 5% of employees worldwide (FY2026), 17,000 in 2021 (Teamsters). No union count or share is stated in Walmart, Target, Home Depot or FedEx 10-Ks I could read, and FedEx Freight is stated as non-union. Amazon: no certified bargaining unit in the NLRB data except JFK8 (ALU, certified 2022; see labor.md section 5).
5. NLRB case volume (bench_labor_nlrb_counts_by_year.csv; the figures in this paragraph are CA charges only, see the reconciliation at the end of this note). Cases per year by name search, 2024: UPS 385, Amazon 194, Costco 46, Walmart incl. Sam's 22, FedEx 10, Target 5, Home Depot 3. Unfair-labor-practice charges against the employer per 10,000 employees (worldwide 10-K headcount): UPS 7.9, Costco 1.4, Amazon 1.25, Target 0.11, Walmart 0.10, Home Depot 0.06 (2024). Amazon's volume rose from 11 cases in 2019 to 205 in 2022 and has stayed near 195; UPS has been 200-425 a year throughout (a long-unionised employer with a contract-enforcement caseload), so UPS is the only comparator with more NLRB activity than Amazon. Walmart's 2022-2026 count (14-26) is about a tenth of Amazon's on a headcount about 35% larger.

## Method and caveats
- NLRB: nlrb.gov/search/case/<name> (same method as scripts/nlrb_list_crawl.py), pages of 10 saved verbatim. The site repeats the last page when asked past the end, so crawls stop on a page with no new case numbers. UPS needed one search per calendar year (the unfiltered query is too large), run on keywords "parcel" and "ups" for 2009-2026. Cases are matched by regex on the case NAME (Walmart/Wal-Mart/Sam's Club; Target Corporation and Target stores only, not unrelated names; Costco; Home Depot; United Parcel Service/UPS; FedEx/Federal Express). This misses cases filed under subsidiary names (e.g. contractors, Walmart Transportation LLC not named Walmart) and includes charges against unions that name the company in brackets (CB cases; UPS has 1,969 of these). Records are sparse before 2009 (the NLRB site holds filed-case data from about 2009); do not use pre-2009 counts. 2026 is partial (through 2026-10-31). A case count is not a measure of violations or outcomes.
- UPS includes UPS Freight cases (sold to TFI in 2021) and UPS Supply Chain Solutions; FedEx includes FedEx Freight, Ground, Home Delivery and Federal Express Corporation (entity_flag column in bench_labor_nlrb_cases.csv).
- Per-employee rates use worldwide 10-K headcounts, which overstate the US base for Walmart (2.1M vs 1.6M US), UPS, Costco and Home Depot; they favour those companies against Amazon. FedEx has no denominator after FY2021, so its rate is blank. Walmart, Target and Home Depot fiscal years ending Jan-Feb are assigned to the prior calendar year.
- Wage rows: metrics differ (floor, starting range, average of store or supply-chain staff). Walmart's $18.50 and $27.50 rows are from an undated Walmart web page retrieved 2026-10-31 (matched to 2025 in the QCEW comparison; an assumption). The Costco $31 average and $19.50 starting wage rows are from news reports of Costco's contract and announcements (source_type column marks secondary). The UPS $21-$23 and $49 figures come from the Teamsters release and a CBS report of the 2023 national agreement (UPS did not publish a comparable release that I retrieved). Spot checks of the raw files confirmed Costco $31, Home Depot $15, UPS $21, Target $15 (2020) and $24 (2022), Walmart $27.50.
- Maintenance note: while extending the UPS crawl I removed 51 redundant duplicate test pages in data/raw/benchmark/labor/nlrb/wmslice_2012/ (a Walmart 2012 slice test; the site had repeated the last page; p000 kept). The slice confirmed 32 Walmart cases for 2012, matching the main table.

## Gaps
- FedEx: no company-announced warehouse/hub wage found (a Bing search returned only job-board and aggregator pages, not used); no FedEx company-wide employee count after FY2021 and no US-only count; no union share.
- Home Depot: one wage announcement (2023, store and supply chain not split); Target: no supply-chain-specific wage; Walmart: DC range is from a 2023 page, not a dated release for later years; Costco has no warehouse-specific (depot) wage.
- Union data only for UPS and Costco; Walmart, Target, Home Depot and FedEx 10-Ks give none. No NLRB election outcomes were collected for peers (only case counts); no state breakdown.
- No wage data before 2018 for peers, and no 2019-2020 pandemic premiums.
- The comparison is to QCEW all-warehousing (NAICS 493), which itself contains Amazon (OSHA-based lower bound 33.5% of NAICS 493 employment in 2025, bench_warehousing_industry_amazon_share_493.csv), so Amazon is not benchmarked against an Amazon-free baseline.
- Other operators named in the brief (Kroger, Lowe's, GXO, DHL Supply Chain, Ryder) were not covered in this topic.

## NLRB totals reconciled (2026-10-31)
Two sets of 2024 figures were in circulation: the collector summary (UPS 385, Amazon 194, Costco 46, Walmart 22, FedEx 10, Target 5, Home Depot 3) and `bench_labor_nlrb_counts_by_year.csv` (UPS 559, Amazon 238, Costco 50, Walmart 22, FedEx 10, Target 5, Home Depot 3). They are not in conflict; they count different things. Recounted from `bench_labor_nlrb_cases.csv` (peers) and `nlrb_amazon_cases_search_index.csv` (Amazon), year filed 2024:
- The summary figures are CA cases only (unfair labor practice charges against the employer): UPS 385, Amazon 194, Costco 46. This is the `ca_ulp_charges_vs_employer` column.
- `all_cases` adds CB charges against unions that name the employer, representation petitions (RC, RM, RD) and other types. UPS 559 = 385 CA + 163 CB + 10 RC/RM + 1 UC. Amazon 238 = 194 CA + 42 CB + 2 RC. Costco 50 = 46 CA + 2 CB + 2 RC/RM. Walmart, Target, Home Depot and FedEx have CA cases only.
- Used for comparison: the CA column (`ca_ulp_charges_vs_employer`), because it is the numerator of the per-10,000 rate (`ca_per_10k_employees`) and CB charges are filed against unions, not against the employer. `all_cases` is supplementary and is not to be quoted as "charges against the company". Amazon CA is 194 in 2024 against 205 in 2022, 194 in 2023 and 105 in 2025 (partial year).
- One of Amazon's 238 cases for 2024 is flagged as an AWS/data-center entity in `nlrb_amazon_cases.csv` (`entity_class`); its case type was not checked. No data file was changed for this item.
